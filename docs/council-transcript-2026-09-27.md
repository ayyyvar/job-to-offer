# LLM Council transcript — job-to-offer positioning (2026-09-27)

Question: which positioning for job-to-offer as a product? The user proposed 4 pillars: (1) tailored CV / cover letters with zero token costs (on-device + PCC), (2) application tracker with export, (3) all data on the phone, (4) Siri App Intents verdicts. The alternative (draft plan): lead with eligibility checks for cross-border candidates. Context: iOS 27 Siri answers "do I qualify" natively but misses visa/location; Teal/Huntr ~$29/mo; Jobright has a US-only H-1B filter; the on-device model over-scores weak jobs by +21–24 points; cover-letter quality on a 3B model is unproven; PCC needs a managed entitlement.

---

## Round 1 — Advisors

### The Contrarian
All four pillars are marketing copy for features nobody pays for, and he rejected the only defensible one. Fatal flaw: his own eval data — the model over-scores weak jobs by +21–24 points and collapses scores into two patterns. The core promise "trust this verdict" is broken at launch; a fit score that calls Weak jobs Good is worse than no score. Cover letters on a 3B model are unproven and almost certainly generic slop; free ChatGPT writes better ones. "Zero token costs" is a cost structure, not a benefit. Privacy has never sold a job-search tool; job seekers post resumes publicly on purpose. Siri integration is a demo, not a moat — Apple already answers "do I qualify" natively and keeps eating that surface every release. PCC adds a managed-entitlement dependency Apple can deny or delay. The irony: the one thing his pipeline does that Siri, LinkedIn and Teal all fail at is deterministic visa/location/language gating for cross-border candidates — the exact positioning he vetoed as "odd." He killed the differentiator and kept the commodities. Jobright proves demand (US-only, H-1B); the non-US cross-border market is unserved. Missing entirely: distribution — a solo dev with no audience has no acquisition story; "free" plus App Store obscurity equals zero users. Verdict: as pitched, a portfolio project wearing a product costume. Either lead with eligibility gating for cross-border candidates — fixing calibration first — or accept it as a personal tool.

### The First Principles Thinker
The pillars are implementation details, not a product. The real question is "what job does the user hire this app for?" — and the honest answer is triage. "Free AI + privacy + Siri" are all how, not why. Nobody wakes up wanting on-device inference. Privacy is a checkout objection-remover, never a purchase driver in consumer job search. Siri integration is a channel Apple can commoditize next release; building your moat on the platform owner's roadmap is renting, not owning. Rebuild from the ground up: the scarce resource for a job seeker is time per posting. The felt pain: 40 tabs open, no idea which 5 deserve an application. The app's verified strength is exactly that — fetch, extract, gate, score, track — in 10 seconds from the share sheet. That's triage. The user's pushback on "location as hero" is half right: location isn't the product, instant verdict is. But the deterministic gates are the only part with a durable edge — Siri demonstrably misses visa/location, and the model over-scores weak jobs by +21–24, so his differentiator is the Swift code, not the LLM. Which kills Pillar 1: cover letters on a 3B model are unproven, quota-capped, and the crowded end of the market. Position as "the 10-second job triage in your share sheet — knows what Siri doesn't: whether you can legally take the job." Gates as trust, speed as hook, privacy as footnote, Siri as distribution. You're choosing whether to compete on generation (you lose) or on deterministic judgment (you already won).

### The Expansionist
Everyone is debating which niche to shrink into. Wrong frame. The four pillars together are the product. He is not competing with Teal at $29/mo — he is building the first zero-marginal-cost job copilot. Teal, Huntr, LinkedIn pay per token and must charge subscriptions; his inference cost is zero forever. He can charge $4.99 one-time or $9.99/yr and keep 100% margin. Price is the moat, not privacy. "Everything Teal does, for the price of one Teal week, forever" is a headline that writes itself, and App Store editorial loves on-device Foundation Models apps right now — a featuring slot is worth more than a year of marketing. The undervalued asset is the deterministic gate layer: Siri over-scores and misses visa issues; his Swift gates never hallucinate a FAIL. That is the trust layer every AI job tool lacks — and it generalizes into a marketplace of rule packs (H-1B, EU Blue Card, remote-timezone, salary floors). Cross-border job seekers are tens of millions of people incumbents structurally ignore; he IS the customer, sitting in Almaty. The Siri App Intent is the wedge nobody can copy without shipping an iOS app: "Hey Siri, does this job take me?" If Apple features that demo, distribution problem solved. Ship all four pillars as "the job copilot that costs nothing to run and never sees your data," lead marketing with the Siri verdict moment, let cross-border gates be the depth. Upside: the default job app for every non-US-passport engineer on iPhone.

### The Outsider
I'm scrolling the App Store. "Zero token costs" means nothing to me — I don't know what a token is; you're solving your own bill, not mine. Privacy as a pillar for a job tracker? My resume is on LinkedIn, Indeed and forty ATS portals already; privacy is a footnote. CSV export screams "built for one nerd." Siri: I have never once wanted to talk to Siri about a job posting, and Siri already does "do I qualify" natively — pillar 4 competes with a free built-in feature; cut it. What made me sit up: "checks visa/location/language rules against your profile." That is a real, painful, specific problem. I've written cover letters for jobs that turned out to require US citizenship in paragraph 9. Nothing on my phone catches that. That's a headline: "Don't waste a week applying to jobs you can't legally take." The builder thinks it's too narrow — it's the only pillar with a story I retell to a friend. Fit scoring and tracking are retention features underneath; they're what Teal already does, they can't be the hook. Cover letters from an unproven tiny model? Don't promise it — one bad letter and I delete the app. Verdict: lead with eligibility ("apply across borders without landmines"), keep score + tracker as the body, make free/private the pricing note ("no $29/mo"), kill the Siri pillar. Fix the builder-speak: "tokens," "PCC," "CSV," "on-device AI."

### The Executor
Ship the eligibility checker, cut CV generation, and buy the $99 developer account this week — nothing else matters until strangers can install it. Monday morning: pay Apple the $99; without it there is no TestFlight, no testers, no product. Positioning: eligibility gates, not fit scoring. Your scoring is broken (+21–24 over-score, two collapsed patterns) — you cannot sell a number you know is wrong. Your gates are deterministic Swift, tested, and solve a real pain Siri fumbles. Cross-border candidates get a binary answer with a quoted sentence from the posting: defensible today with zero model work. Cut, in order: (1) CV/cover-letter generation — dead on arrival; PCC needs a managed entitlement with an application process, weeks of Apple bureaucracy, and a 4096-token on-device model writes bad cover letters; punt entirely. (2) Fit score as a headline — demote to "estimate" or hide behind a toggle. (3) Siri App Intents — nice demo, ships week 4, not week 1; one intent only ("check this job" → PASS/FAIL/FLAG). Sequence: week 1 — $99 account + onboarding form replacing the hard-coded profile (6 fields); week 2 — TestFlight with 10 testers from cross-border job-seeker communities (Almaty/CIS relocation chats — he already lives in this market); weeks 3–4 — fix what testers break, add the one Siri intent. "Free AI + privacy" is a feature list, not a product. "Know in 5 seconds if you're wasting an application" is a product.

## Chairman's verdict

**Where the council agrees:** the 4 proposed pillars are "how", not "why" (tokens/privacy/on-device do not drive downloads); cover letters are the weakest bet (3B model unproven, PCC entitlement gated, ChatGPT free); the deterministic gates are the only durable asset; the fit score cannot be sold as-is (+21–24 over-score); privacy is a pricing footnote.

**Where the council clashes:** Siri (cut it vs it's the uncopyable wedge — resolved as one intent in week 4); breadth vs depth (Expansionist's "ship all four" was named the biggest blind spot by 4 of 5 reviewers); eligibility as headline (resolved by First Principles: the product is the instant verdict, the gates are the proof inside it, not the name).

**Blind spots caught in review:** the cost of a wrong verdict (quote as proof, "not sure — check yourself" state, trust earned on the first 3 jobs); the onboarding cliff (a user-editable profile is the biggest unscoped build); retention economics (users churn in 8–12 weeks; nobody sized the market); distribution (users search "job tracker"; the real channel is CIS/relocation communities); the consumer-app premise itself (the rule engine could be a B2B API).

**Recommendation:** hook = instant verdict with proof ("know in 10 seconds if you're wasting an application — including whether you can legally take the job"); body = tracker (retention); pricing note = free, no server, no $29/mo; Siri = one intent as demo and channel; CV/cover letters = out of V1.

**One thing first:** buy the $99 Apple Developer account and replace the hard-coded profile with a 6-field form. Without TestFlight and an editable profile there is no product under any positioning.

**Chairman's addition:** the tracker is undervalued — the verdict hooks, the tracker retains. If retention does not appear after the profile form and 10 testers, the B2B path (rule engine as an API) stays open with the same code.

**User's decision (2026-09-27):** accepted. Remove the hard-coded profile; support uploading multiple CVs. Plan rewritten in `docs/PRODUCT_PLAN.md`.
